Problem 5.1

(a) I = I₀/2

Reasoning written alongside:

    E = E₀ sin(kz − ωt) x̂ + E₀ cos(kz − ωt) ŷ

After the polarizer only one component remains, e.g. E₀ sin(kz − ωt) x̂.

Answer: I = I₀/2

(b) Δt · Δν ≈ 1

    Δν = 10⁹ Hz ~ 1 GHz

Answer: bandwidth ~ 1 GHz

(c) v_p = ω/k = √(g/k)

    v_g = dω/dk = √g / (2√k)

    v_p = 2 v_g

Answer: v_p = √(g/k), v_g = √g/(2√k), so v_p = 2v_g

(d) Width of the diffractive peak is at ≈ (πD/λ) sin ψ = π

    sin ψ = R/L = λ/D  ⇒  R = (λ/D)·L = (5·10⁻⁷ / 1) · 3.8·10⁸ = 200 m

More precise radius is obtained by analysing circular sources:

    r = (1.22 λ / D) · R = 230 m

Answer: spot radius ≈ 200 m from the simple estimate, ≈ 230 m using the 1.22λ/D
circular-aperture result.

(e) Light reflected from the water surface is predominantly polarized with E⃗
parallel to the water surface. Polaroid glasses are filtering out this
polarization. By rotating by 90° we let them in, negating the sun-blocking effect.

(f) Use

    sin²(Nδ/2) / sin²(δ/2)

    N·d = const, d smaller  ⇒  distance between principal maxima increases;
    width smaller, height larger.

    λ larger  ⇒  distance between principal maxima increases.

Answer: more lines per cm (smaller d) pushes the principal maxima further apart and
makes each one narrower and taller; longer wavelength also pushes the principal
maxima further apart.

Problem 5.2

(a) B⃗ ⊥ k⃗ ,  ∇⃗·B⃗ = 0

    ∂Bx/∂x + ∂By/∂y + ∂Bz/∂z = 0

    0 + ∂/∂y (B₀ cos(kz − ωt)) + u · ∂/∂z (B₀ cos(kz − ωt)) = 0

with the annotations: the first term is 0, the second term (∂/∂y) = 0, and the
bracket multiplying u is ≠ 0.

    ⇒ u = 0 !

    B⃗(r⃗, t) = B₀ ŷ cos(kz − ωt)

Answer: u = 0

(b) E⃗(r⃗, t) = B₀ · c x̂ cos(kz − ωt)

(c) F⃗ = q(E⃗ + v⃗ × B⃗),  a⃗ = F⃗/m   [written as a⃗ = E⃗/m in the scan; the q/m is
supplied on the next line]

Force due to B⃗ field can be ignored since q is constrained to move along x̂.

    a⃗ = qE⃗/m = (q B₀ c / m) x̂ cos(ωt)   since z = 0 !

(d)

    E⃗_rad = − q a⃗⊥(t − r/c) / (4π ε₀ r c²)

    1) r⃗ = r x̂  ⇒  E⃗_rad = 0 , a⃗ is along x̂

    2) r⃗ = r ŷ  ⇒  E⃗_rad = − q² B₀ c cos(t − r/c) / (4π ε₀ r c²) · x̂

       Linear polarization

    3) r⃗ = r ẑ   SAME AS IN 2)

[Note: in 2) the scan writes cos(t − r/c) without an explicit ω inside the cosine,
and carries q² · B₀ c / (4πε₀ r c²) — the m from a⃗ = qB₀c/m does not appear in the
handwritten numerator. Transcribed as written.]

Problem 5.3

(a) [Figure: wavefronts arriving along the grating. The incoming rays are drawn
crossing the grating plane; θ is marked between the incoming ray and the grating
normal, d is the slit separation marked along the grating, and ψ is marked between
the outgoing ray and the horizontal axis. The extra path length Δℓ is the short
segment at the foot of the diagram, with an arrow pointing to it.]

    δ_after = Δℓ · k = (2π/λ) · d sin ψ

    Δℓ = d sin ψ

(b) Incoming wave will introduce add[itional] phase delay. We defined θ and ψ such
that phases cancel each other. In addition the presence of glass changes phase
shift magnitude.

    Δδ_before = − (2πd/λ′) sin θ = − (2π d n / λ₀) sin θ ,   λ′ = (1/n) λ₀

    δ_TOT = (2πd/λ₀) (sin ψ − n sin θ)

(c)

    I = I₀ · sin²( (5πd/λ₀)(sin ψ − n sin θ) ) / sin²( (πd/λ₀)(sin ψ − n sin θ) )

where I₀ is the intensity from a single slit.

[In the scan a "25" is written above I₀ and struck through — the ratio itself
already gives 25 at the principal maxima.]

(d) [Figure: sketch of I versus sin ψ. Three tall principal maxima are drawn, with
four small secondary maxima between each adjacent pair. The central principal
maximum is labelled 25 I₀ and its position is marked n sin θ on the horizontal
axis. The horizontal axis is labelled sin ψ. An arrow points to the first zero just
to the right of the central peak.]

    Primary max at δ = 0

    1st minimum at 5δ/2 = π   ⇒   sin ψ = n sin θ + λ/(5d)


(e) N → 3

    I = 9 I₀ · sin²( (3πd/λ)(sin ψ − n sin θ) ) / sin²( (πd/λ)(sin ψ − n sin θ) )

[The scan writes the prefactor as 9 I₀ here, and uses λ rather than λ₀.]

(f) [Figure: sketch of I versus sin ψ for the 3-slit case. Principal maxima are
drawn with only a single small secondary maximum between adjacent principal maxima,
and the peaks are visibly broader than in part (d). The central principal maximum
is labelled 9 I₀ and its position is marked n sin θ; the horizontal axis is
labelled sin ψ. An arrow points to the first minimum just right of the central
peak.]

    Primary max at δ = 0

    1st minimum at 3δ/2 = π   ⇒   sin ψ = n sin θ + λ/(3d)

Comparison:

    Max value              25 I₀  ↔  9 I₀
    Primary max pos.       same
    1st minimum            + λ/(5d)   ↔   + λ/(3d)

1st minimum further out for 3.
P.M. wider for 3.

Answer: blocking the two outer slits drops the principal-maximum intensity from
25 I₀ to 9 I₀, leaves the positions of the principal maxima unchanged, and moves
the first minimum from n sin θ + λ/(5d) out to n sin θ + λ/(3d), i.e. the principal
maxima become wider.
