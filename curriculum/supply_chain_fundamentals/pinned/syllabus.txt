CTL.SC1x Supply Chain Fundamentals (Term 3T2026)

Program:
Part of the MITx MicroMasters Credential in Supply Chain Management (SCM), offered by MIT's Center for Transportation & Logistics. This is an open-enrollment course, free to audit, with a paid Certificate (Verified) track.

Course Description:
Supply chains are complex, global enterprises that require both analytical and practical perspectives to balance efficiency with flexibility and resilience. SC1x provides the foundational skills required for supply chain management and logistics by introducing the analytical tools and techniques used to design and operate logistics systems and integrated supply chains. Through mathematical models, hands-on applications, and real-world industry examples, you learn to master three critical supply chain functions: demand forecasting, inventory management, and transportation planning and execution. By the end of the course you are equipped to improve overall supply chain performance and strategically reduce total costs. SC1x is an instructor-paced course; content is released weekly.

Learning Objectives:
- Describe supply chain management and logistics fundamentals, and the trade-offs among cost, service, flexibility, and resilience.
- Build and apply demand forecasting models: time-series analysis, exponential smoothing, seasonality, and special cases.
- Apply inventory management models: the total cost equation and Economic Order Quantity (EOQ), single-period (newsvendor) models, and multi-period continuous- and periodic-review policies.
- Analyze the fundamentals of freight transportation, mode selection, and freight transportation cost analysis.
- Use spreadsheets to model, solve, and interpret supply chain problems.

Prerequisites:
No formal prerequisites. Because SC1x is quantitative, learners benefit from comfort with algebra, spreadsheets, and introductory statistics and probability.

Weekly Topics:
- Week 1 — Supply Chain Management: Core Concepts. Introduces supply chain management and logistics, the key functions and flows, and how the pieces fit together; sets up the analytical mindset used through the course.
- Week 2 — Forecasting I: Time Series Analysis and Exponential Smoothing. Time-series components, naive and moving-average methods, and exponential smoothing; measuring and interpreting forecast error.
- Week 3 — Forecasting II: Seasonality and Special Cases. Extending exponential smoothing to trend and seasonality (Holt and Holt-Winter methods) and handling special forecasting cases.
- Week 4 — Inventory I: Total Cost Equation and EOQ. The total cost equation and the Economic Order Quantity model, including sensitivity and practical extensions.
- Week 5 — Prep Week: Catch up and review in preparation for the Midterm.
- Week 6 — Midterm Exam: Assesses Weeks 1–4.
- Week 7 — Inventory II: Single Period Inventory Models. The newsvendor / single-period model under stochastic demand; critical ratio and optimal order quantity.
- Week 8 — Inventory III: Multi-period Inventory Models and Warehousing. Continuous-review and periodic-review policies, safety stock, and service levels; warehousing considerations.
- Week 9 — Transportation I: Fundamentals of Freight Transportation. Transportation stakeholders, modes, and cost structures.
- Week 10 — Transportation II: Freight Transportation Analysis and Case Study. Mode selection and freight transportation cost analysis, applied through the Clean'N Green case study.
- Week 12 — Final Exam.

Assessment & Grading:
The course separates a learning space (videos, quick questions, and practice problems with immediate feedback; weekly Graded Assignments as formative assessments, with solutions released after the due date) from an assessment space (the Midterm and Final Exams, which evaluate individual mastery; no solutions or individualized feedback are provided for exam questions). To pass the course and earn a Certificate, you must achieve a final course grade of at least 60%. Grade weights:
- Weekly Graded Assignments: 20%
- Midterm Exam: 35%
- Final Exam: 45%

Enrollment Track Access:
Graded Assignments for Weeks 1, 2, and 3 are available to both Audit and Certificate Track learners. Graded Assignments for Weeks 4, 7, 8, 9, and 10, the Midterm Exam, and the Final Exam are available only to Certificate Track learners.
