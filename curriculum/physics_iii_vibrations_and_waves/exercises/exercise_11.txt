Problem 1.1 (30 pts)

Consider mass M hanging on a massless vertical spring with spring constant k (see Figure 1). The mass is at rest. At t = 0 a piece of the mass falls off, leaving only a fraction α of the original mass attached to the spring. Gravitational acceleration is g. Assume that the mass moves along the vertical y-axis. At t = 0 the mass was at y = 0.

[Figure 1: Broken Mass — a vertical y-axis points upward, with the origin y = 0 marked by a tick partway up. A horizontal rigid ceiling at the top supports a coil spring of spring constant k that hangs straight down along the axis. The bottom of the spring attaches to the top of a rectangular block whose top face sits at y = 0. The block is divided by a horizontal dashed line: the upper portion, labelled αM, is the fraction that remains attached to the spring; the whole block, labelled M, is the original mass before the lower piece falls off at t = 0.]

(a) Find the new equilibrium position as a function of the given parameters.
(b) The mass will oscillate. What is the period of oscillations?
(c) What is the time dependence of the vertical position, y(t)?
(d) What is the amplitude and phase of the motion in terms of given parameters?
(e) What is the kinetic and potential energy of mass m as a function of time?


Problem 1.2 (30 pts)

Consider a simple oscillator with damping. Mass m is attached to a spring of spring constant k and a damper with damping force proportional to −bv. The spring and the damper are attached to the walls on the opposite sides of the mass (see Figure 2). The oscillator can be driven either by moving an attachment point on the damper (A) or the end of the spring (B). In both cases the position of the attachment point as a function of time is s(t) = s₀ cos(ω_d t). For BOTH of the above cases, A and B, answer each of the following questions.

[Figure 2: Two ways to drive an oscillator — two horizontal one-dimensional sketches of the same oscillator, drawn one above the other. In each sketch, reading left to right along a horizontal line: an attachment point marked with a small open circle and labelled A; a dashpot (damper) labelled b; a rigid rod to a rectangular block labelled m; a coil spring labelled k; and a second attachment point marked with a small open circle and labelled B. So the damper connects the mass to the left-hand point A and the spring connects the mass to the right-hand point B. In the upper sketch a double-headed horizontal arrow labelled s(t) sits under point A, indicating that the damper's attachment point A is the one being driven back and forth while B is held fixed. In the lower sketch the identical double-headed arrow labelled s(t) sits under point B instead, indicating that the spring's end B is driven while A is held fixed.]

(a) Write the equations of motion of the mass m.
(b) Find the amplitude of steady state solution in terms of given parameters. Sketch the oscillation amplitude as a function of frequency ω_d.
(c) What is the frequency ω_d at which the amplitude is at a maximum?
(d) What is the behavior of the amplitude at ω_d ≪ ω₀ and ω_d ≫ ω₀?


Problem 1.3 (40 pts)

Consider the five different coupled oscillating systems in Figure 3 A-E. For each system please write a set of equations of motion for small amplitude harmonic oscillations. Define clearly the coordinates that you are using to describe each system. The equations should be written in matrix form: (A − ω²I)C = 0 where, for the familiar systems consisting of masses and springs, A = M^(−1)K. There is also an equivalent matrix for LC circuits. DO NOT find the solutions to these equations!

Notes for the systems:

A. The ends of the springs which are not attached to a mass are fixed.
B. Ignore vertical motion of the masses.
C. Ignore effects due to the curvature of the ring.
D. Write the equations of motion in terms of the currents through each of the three inductors.
E. Consider only small-angle oscillations.

[Figure 3: Five Oscillators — five separate systems, each tagged by a circled letter.

A. A horizontal chain between two rigid walls. Reading left to right: left wall, a spring k, a block of mass m, a spring k, a block of mass 3m, a spring k, a block of mass m, a spring k, right wall. Four springs, all of constant k; three masses m, 3m, m in that order; both outer spring ends are fixed to the walls. Motion is horizontal.

B. Three blocks hanging from a common rigid horizontal ceiling, each on its own string of length L, so each block is a pendulum. The three blocks all have mass m. Adjacent hanging blocks are connected to each other by horizontal springs: a spring of constant k joins the left block to the middle block, and a spring of constant 3k joins the middle block to the right block. The outermost sides of the left and right blocks are free — no springs run to any wall. Motion is horizontal (vertical motion of the masses is ignored).

C. A closed circular ring made entirely of spring, with four point masses, each of mass m, mounted on the ring at the four positions upper-left, upper-right, lower-left, lower-right, dividing the ring into four spring segments. The segment along the top, joining the upper-left and upper-right masses, has spring constant k. The segment down the right side, joining the upper-right and lower-right masses, has spring constant 3k. The segment along the bottom, joining the lower-left and lower-right masses, has spring constant k. The segment up the left side, joining the lower-left and upper-left masses, has spring constant 3k. The masses are free to move along the ring (tangentially), and the curvature of the ring is to be ignored, so the system behaves as four masses on a closed loop with springs k, 3k, k, 3k around it.

D. An LC ladder circuit. There are four vertical branches side by side, each containing a capacitor of capacitance C. The bottoms of all four capacitors connect to a common horizontal wire (the bottom rail). The tops of adjacent capacitors are joined by inductors: an inductor L between the tops of capacitors 1 and 2, another inductor L between the tops of capacitors 2 and 3, and a third inductor L between the tops of capacitors 3 and 4. So the circuit has four capacitors C and three inductors L, and the currents through the three inductors are the coordinates to be used.

E. A triple pendulum hanging in a chain from a rigid horizontal ceiling. A string of length L runs from the ceiling to a point mass m; a second string of length L runs from that mass down to a second point mass m; a third string of length L runs from the second mass down to a third point mass m. All three masses are equal to m and all three string segments have the same length L. The chain is drawn slightly displaced from vertical, and only small-angle oscillations are to be considered.]
