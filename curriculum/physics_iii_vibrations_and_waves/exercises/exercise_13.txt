Problem 3.1 (16 pts)

The electric field for a TE mode in an infinitely long (in the x direction) perfectly conducting rectangular waveguide (a < b) is given by

    E⃗(x,y,z,t) = E₀ cos(k_y y + φ_y) cos(k_x x − ωt) ẑ

[Figure 1: A perfectly conducting waveguide — a rectangular box drawn in perspective with its long axis along x, extending indefinitely to the right (the open end is drawn with dotted lines and a wavy field pattern inside). The y-axis points up and the z-axis points toward the viewer, both from the origin at the near-left corner. The height of the box along y is labelled a, and the depth along z is labelled b, with a < b. The guide is infinitely long in the x direction.]

(a) Find k_y and φ_y that satisfy the boundary conditions.
(b) Write down the dispersion relation for this mode of the waveguide.
(c) What is the lowest frequency that will propagate in this mode?
(d) What is the magnetic field B⃗(x,y,z,t) associated with the electric field of this mode?

Problem 3.6 (18 pts)

A charged particle of mass M and charge +Q is attached to the end of a spring of spring constant k. The spring lies along the x-axis and the equilibrium point is at the origin. The particle is displaced from equilibrium by a distance A in the x direction, and released at t = 0. Assume that the size of the particle is much smaller than A, so it can be treated as a point charge, and that the damping rate is very small.

[Figure 10: An oscillating charge — a vertical wall on the left with a horizontal coil spring of constant k attached to it, extending to the right. At the free end of the spring is a circular particle labelled Q,M. Axes are drawn above the spring: z points up and x points to the right, with the origin at the particle's equilibrium position. The particle oscillates along the x-axis.]

(a) Calculate the electric field radiated by the particle along an arbitrary direction in the x−z plane, at a distance R, where R ≫ A.
(b) Calculate the total time averaged power radiated by the particle.
(c) Assuming that the power radiated does not change appreciably as a function of time, give a simple rough estimate of the time it will take for the particle to decrease its amplitude of oscillation to 1/e of its initial value. Is this assumption realistic?
(d) A more refined estimate can be obtained using that dA/dt = (dA/dE) × (dE/dt), and using the average power radiated over a given cycle for dE/dt. Use this to calculate the time it will take the particle to decrease its oscillation amplitude to 1/e of its initial value.
