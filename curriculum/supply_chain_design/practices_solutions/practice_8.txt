Practice Problem 1: New Forklifts

Part 1 — Correct answer: Long term assets (PPE) increase by $250,000 and short term assets (Cash) decrease by $250,000
Explanation: Option number 4 is correct. The balance sheet reports the assets and liabilities of a company. This is where we see which assets the company currently has and how these assets were financed. So, when the company purchases new productive assets using cash, we see a reduction in the Cash-position, and an increase in the Long term assets position.

Part 2 — Correct answer: No impact
Explanation: The correct answer is that there is no impact. We do not see any change in the income statement at the year of purchase as we assume that not enough time has passed since the purchase for the asset to lose any value. Once the asset starts losing value – that is, the value depreciates – then this loss of value is reported in the income statement.

Part 3 — Correct answer: Operating costs increase by $22,500 and net income before taxes decreases by $22,500
Explanation: The year after purchase we will see a reduction in the value of the asset. Since the company is assuming linear deprecation over ten years, the reduction in value is ($250,000-$25,000)/10=$22,500. The $25,000 is the residual value after ten years. The loss of value is considered an expense, so in the income statement Costs increase by $22,500 and net income before taxes decrease by $22,500.

Part 4 — Correct answer: 2350000
Explanation: Explanation

        When we make the purchase, our long term assets increase. As the value of the forklifts decrease, so does the long term assets. After ten years, the fork lifts are no longer depreciating, and remain on their salvage value (or “scrap value”) of $25,000. So our long term assets would be 2,325,000+25,000=2,350,000.

Part 5 — Correct answer: The taxable net income will be lower in the first years but higher in the later years
Explanation: When the asset depreciates at a higher rate, the expenses are also higher. When the expenses are greater, net income is smaller. This means that with an accelerated depreciation, the company postpones tax payments for later years.

Practice Problem 2: Cost Accounting at Youkea

Part 1 — Correct answer: Warehouse personnel
Explanation: An activity is usually expressed as a verb plus a noun. Whether an activity is relevant depends on whether or not it is important for allocating costs in a way that represents the resource consumption. In fact, the following five activities are the activities that should be considered based on what we know:
        Process phone orders
        Process web orders
        Handle chairs in warehouse
        Ship Common carrier
        Ship Express delivery

Part 2 — Correct answer: 3000 | 110000
Explanation: We now have to look at the information we have about how much resources different activities consume.
      Process phone orders: Total order entry expenses are $20,000, and from the text we see that phone orders consume 85% of these costs. This gives us a total cost of $20,000*0.85=$17,000 for this activity.
      Process web orders: The web orders consume the remaining 15% of the order entry costs, so the total cost is $3,000.
      Handle chairs in warehouse: This activity represents all warehouse acitvities, so we allocate all warehouse overhead here: $400,000+$40,000=$440,000.
      Ship Common carrier: This activity consumes all of the freight costs for common carrier and nothing else: $350,000.
      Ship Express delivery: This activity has both shipment costs and personnel costs: $100,000+10,000=$110,000.

Part 3 — Correct answer: Activity "Handle chairs in warehouse" has cost driver "number of chairs"
Explanation: Ok, so this is a very important step in the analysis where it is easy to make a mistake - be careful!
        1. No, we read in the text that for the web order activity, costs are driven by number of orders
        2. Yes, this is a fair assumption based on the information the warehouse costs are driven by throughput
        3. No, "warehouse personnel" is not an activity, it is a cost
        4. No, the costs for the activity "Ship Common carrier" are driven by the number of chairs shipped
        5. No, item number 2 is correct
        The correct drivers are the following:
        Process phone orders: # order lines
        Process web orders: # web orders
        Handle chairs in warehouse: # ordered chairs
        Ship Common carrier: # chairs shipped by common carrier
        Ship Express delivery: # express deliveries

Part 4 — Correct answer: 5.36 | 1100.00
Explanation: To get this we need to first extract from the information how many units we have in total of each driver. For instance, for web orders we need to find the total number of web orders (our driver from Part 3). We see in the text that 20% of orders are received over the phone, so 80% of the 700 orders are web orders, which gives us a total of 560 web orders. We then find the driver rate by taking the total cost from Part 2 (which was found to be $3,000) and divide by the total number of orders.
      The correct driver rates are the following:
      Process phone orders: $17,000/800=$21.25 per order line
      Process web orders: $3,000/560=5.36 per web order
      Handle chairs in warehouse: $440,000/4,800=$91.67 per ordered chair
      Ship Common carrier: $350,000/4,000=$87.50 per chair on common carrier
      Ship Express delivery: $110,000/100 = $1,100 per express delivery

Part 5 — Correct answer: 44914 | 55142
Explanation: To find this we multiply our driver rates with the customer specific values.
      For Customer A:
      Process phone orders: $85
      Process web orders: $37.50
      Handle chairs in warehouse: $22,917
      Ship Common carrier: $21,875
      Ship Express delivery: 0
      TOTAL: $44,914
      For Customer B
      Process phone orders: $850
      Process web orders: $0
      Handle chairs in warehouse: $22,917
      Ship Common carrier: $14,875
      Ship Express delivery: $16,500
      TOTAL: $55,142

Part 6 — Correct answer: 8.17 | 633
Explanation: According to the ABC exercise, the cost to serve Customer B is really $221 per chair. So the cost per chair, including direct costs, is $221+$330=$551. The margin is then really (600-551)/600=8.17% (roughly). (Note, this should not be confused with profit percentage, which is (600-551)/551=8.39%)
      So what does this tell us? Well, Youkea's way of allocating costs has been misleading, and Customer B is not as profitable as Youkea thinks. In fact, they need to increase the price by 5.5% (to $633) to make the margin they think they are making. The smaller Customer A is actually more profitable (if a similar pricing scheme is used)!

Practice Problem 3: Costing of Metal Widgets

Part 1 — Correct answer: 77 | 51 | 62
Explanation: Ok, so now we have to first find the overhead rate, and then add this to the direct costs for each widget.
      First we find the overhead rate, by taking total overhead and divide by total labor hours: $150,000/3040=$49.34 per hour. Now, we know the labor hours per batch for the three products, so we can easily calculate the cost per batch:
      Gold: $49.3*100=$4,934
      Silver: $49.3*65=$3,207
      Bronze: $49.3*90=$4,440
      Dividing these numbers by 100 we get the overhead cost per unit. We then add the direct costs:
      Gold=$49+15+13=$77
      Silver=$32+14+5=$51
      Bronze=$44+10+8=$62

Part 2 — Correct answer: 321 | 647
Explanation: The rates are per hour of operation.
      For the Setup activity, we have total cost of $75,000 and total activity of 234 hours, leading to $75,000/234 hours= $321 per hour of setup.
      For the inspection activity, we get $647 per hour of inspection.

Part 3 — Correct answer: 64 | 92
Explanation: We now need to remember that we have information on batches. We have 1000/100=10 batches of Gold planned for the coming week. Every batch has a setup time of 10 hours, so total setup time for Gold is 100 hours. Every batch has inspection time of 5 hours, so total inspection time for Gold is 50 hours.
      Setup cost per unit: $321*100/1,000 units=$32 per unit
      Inspection cost per unit: $647*50/1,000 units=$32 per unit
      Overhead per unit for Gold is then= $32+$32=$64 per unit
      Adding direct costs we have: $64+$13+$15=$92
      Note that this is much higher than the traditional approach. Why? Because the ABC system allocates overhead based on how much each product uses the common resources. The traditional approach underestimates the cost of the Gold widget by some 15% because it is only considering direct labor hours. Looking at the data, we see that labor hours are not very representative of how much each of the different products use the common resources - the Gold widget consumes as many hours doing setups and inspections as the other two products taken together, whereas labor hours are almost the same.

Practice Problem 4: Cost accounting at RCH Industries

Part 1 — Correct answer: 760 | 480
Explanation: The relevant costs for the first activity are the Personnel (warehousing and storage) costs ($800), and the Facility and equipment costs ($200). We know that Stylish accounts for 80% of all personnel time in the warehouse and 60% of the equipment hours at the warehouse. Since we operate at full capacity, we can calculate the total cost for the activity of warehousing and storing Stylish:
      800*0.8+200*0.6=$760
      The relevant costs for the second activity are the Personnel (shipment processing and loading) costs ($600), and the Shipment processing costs ($200). We know that EHV accounts for 60% of outbound shipment processing and personnel cost. We can calculate the total cost for the activity of shipment processing and loading EHV:
      600*0.6+200*0.6=$480

Part 2 — Correct answer: 2.53 | 10.67
Explanation: We now want to allocate the total cost of the activities onto the drivers.
      Warehousing and storage Leisure incurs a total cost of $240. From the text we see that there are 95 totes with Leisure items. We get that the cost per tote is:
      $240/95= 2.53
      Shipment processing and loading LTL incurs a total cost of $320. There are 30 LTL shipments per week. We get that the cost per shipment is
      $320/30 = 10.67

Part 3 — Correct answer: 882 | 486
Explanation: For customer A we get that:
      13.82*20 +2.53*10 +16*20 +10.67*3 +28.57*8=882
      For customer B we get that:
      13.82*12 +2.53*18 +16*8 +10.67*3 +28.57*4=486
      Using ABC costing, Customer A is almost twice as expensive to serve as Customer B. Why? Well, while the order the same volume, Customer A has a larger portion of Stylish, which is more expensive to handle, and a large portion of EHV deliveries, which are more expensive to load. On top of this, Customer A has more frequent deliveries!

Part 4 — Correct answer: 560 | 560
Explanation: To find this we take the total overhead ($2800) and divide by the total number of totes (150). With then multiply by the number of totes for each customer. Since the number of totes are the same, we get the same cost to serve! What does this mean? Well, since we are allocating overhead based on sales volume, the two customers look identical in terms of cost since they have the same volume. This is very different from the cost we get by investigating how of the resources the two customers consume.

Part 5 — Correct answer: 640 | 320
Explanation: To find this we take the total overhead ($2800) and divide by the total number of shipments (35). We then multiply by the number of shipments for each customer.
      And yes, they are directionally correct. This provides a more fair result -- customer A is definitely more expensive. However, as we can see, this allocation definitely underestimates the cost-to-serve for both customers since their share of shipments does not reflect their overall resource consumption.

Practice Problem 5: The Cash-to-Cash Cycle for Consumer Goods Companies

Part 1 — Correct answer: 37 | 59 | 58
Explanation: The DIO is found by taking the average inventory (in dollars) over the COGS, times 365 days. Average inventory is found as (starting inventory + ending inventory)/2.

Part 2 — Correct answer: 37 | 28 | 44
Explanation: The DSO is found by taking the average accounts receivables (in dollars) over total sales, times 365 days. Average accounts receivables is found as (starting A/R + ending A/R)/2.

Part 3 — Correct answer: 110 | 74 | 73
Explanation: The DPO is found by taking the average accounts payables (in dollars) over COGS, times 365 days. Average accounts payables is found as (starting A/P + ending A/P)/2.

Part 4 — Correct answer: -36 | 13 | 29
Explanation: The CCC is found by: CCC=DIO+DSO-DPO

Part 5 — Correct answer: Unilever needs fewer days of funding to run its business compared to Kimberly-Clark; On average, Kimberly-Clark pays their suppliers faster than Unilever; On average, Procter and Gamble has shorter payment terms for its customers compared to Kimberly-Clark
Explanation: 1. False: Procter and Gamble has a greater DIO, so it would take them longer to deplete its inventory – 59 days compared to 37 days for Unilever
        2. True: on average, Procter and Gamble sales are collected after 28 days, while Kimberly-Clark sales are collected after 44 days
        3. True: on average, Kimberly-Clarke pays suppliers after 73 days, while Unilever pays after 110 days
        4. True: Unilever has a negative Cash-to-cash-Cycle, so it can fund purchases from suppliers using cash from customers
        5. False as per above
