the simple concepts (converting to common units, translating times from months to years, etc.).  So, let's make sure we
are all comfortable with these basic ideas before moving on to the more complicated concepts.   So, for this problem it is
all about the basics, with no trouble (I hope).

Part 2
When plugging values into an EOQ equation, it is important to have consistency across their units. It is important to
remember that this applies also to units of time, like weeks, months and years. In a single equation, it is important to
input values expressed in the same units, i.e. all in terms of years, or all in terms of months, but not a mix of months and
years without the proper conversion factors.
Consider the following example. A certain product has a demand of 240 units per month. It has a holding charge of 12%
of the item cost per year.
