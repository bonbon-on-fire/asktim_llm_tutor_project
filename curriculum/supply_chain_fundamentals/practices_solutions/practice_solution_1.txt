
At the bagel shop, you have some cakes that you sell that are pretty slow movers.  The chocolate cake, for example, only
sells on average 2.5 cakes per week.  You have noticed that the demand follows a Poisson distribution.
Part 1

Enter your answer in decimal form using two decimal places. For example, if your answer is 23.24%, you should enter 0.23 in the box below.
Answer: 0.24
Part 2
What is the probability that you will sell no cakes in a week?
Enter your answer in decimal form using three decimal places. For example, if your answer is 23.24%, you should enter 0.232 in the box below.
Answer: 0.082
Part 3
Suppose you have 5 cakes made ready to sell. What is the probability that you will sell all your cakes in one week?
Enter your answer in decimal form using three decimal places. For example, if your answer is 23.24%, you should enter 0.232 in the box below.
Answer: 0.109
Explanation
Using the Poisson Table, we want to find the probability that we sell up to 3 cakes for lambda = 2.5. This is 0.7576. So, the
probability that I will sell more than 3 cakes = 1 - 0.7576 = 0.2424 or about 24%. Alternatively, we can use the formula 1-
POISSON(3,2.5,true) to obtain the same answer in a spreadsheet.
