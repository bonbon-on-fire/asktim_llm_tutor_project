Course description

This course is aimed at the aspiring planning practitioner, policy-maker, or industry decision-maker with an interest in urban transportation and environmental issues in Latin America. It focuses on current transport-related themes confronting many cities in the region, including rapid motorization and suburbanization and their impacts on transportation infrastructure and quality of life; public sector management and improvement of privately-owned and operated transit systems; and transportation air pollution problems and potential solutions. The course is geared toward interactive problem-solving: Part I delivers lectures on the principal issues surrounding transportation in the developing world (motorization, fiscal pressures, urban sprawl), concepts of sustainability as they relate to urban transportation, regional strategic planning approaches, and transportation policy and technology options, while Part II applies that knowledge to two case studies — Mexico City and Santiago de Chile — in which student teams develop strategic solutions to real transport-land use-environment challenges. This is MIT course 11.943J / ESD.935, taught by Prof. Pericles (Chris) Zegras in the Department of Urban Studies and Planning and the Engineering Systems Division (Graduate, Spring 2002).
