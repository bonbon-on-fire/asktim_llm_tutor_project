TITLE: Chef Yourself — Case Analysis (Tutor)

## Your assignment
You are stepping into the role of Wendy Swenson, newly hired as the first Supply Chain Director of Chef Yourself. The COO, Raj Singh, has given you two weeks to come back with a PRIORITIZED list of initiatives that (1) reduce the total landed cost of a box of meals and (2) improve the level of service. Read the case below and work toward a defensible, prioritized recommendation — what you would tackle first, why, and roughly how large the prize is.

Use this session to think the case through with the tutor. You should do the analysis and the writing; the tutor is here to question your reasoning and help you get unstuck, not to hand you the answer.

Questions to work through:
1. Where is the total landed cost of a box actually going, and which cost components have grown the most over the last year? Build up the per-box cost picture from the facts below.
2. Why did expanding into the four new cities RAISE cost per box instead of lowering it? What would you have expected, and what actually drives the gap?
3. Which root causes are shared across the silos (forecasting, promotions, single kitchen, transportation mode) versus local to one function?
4. What is your prioritized list of initiatives, and what is your reasoning for the ordering? Which are quick wins vs. structural bets? Which depend on cooperation between functions that currently distrust each other?
5. Is the plan feasible given a single Sacramento kitchen already at capacity and an aggressive timeline?

## The situation
Chef Yourself delivers "partially prepared" meal kits (individually packaged raw ingredients + recipes) to customers' homes. Founded 2008 in Sacramento by Jon Bush; grew by word of mouth, then online. In 2014 it took outside funding, renovated/expanded the single Sacramento kitchen, and expanded from 3 established markets to 4 new cities. As of spring 2016 it serves six metro areas:
- Established markets: Sacramento (SA), San Francisco (SF), San Jose (SJ)
- New markets: Los Angeles (LA), San Diego (SD), Las Vegas (LV), Phoenix (PH)

The core problem: the total landed cost of a box (three meals) has risen continuously and is now ~50% higher than two years ago. Growth into the four new cities raised cost per box instead of lowering it. The single Sacramento kitchen is already at capacity, and service levels are starting to slip. The company posted an operating LOSS of ~$2.83M on sales over $54M this year, versus ~5% operating margins before the expansion.

Ordering/production rhythm: menus published one month ahead; customers order up to two weeks before delivery (about 20% order ~4 weeks out). Order deadline is one week before production; once frozen, sourcing finalizes purchases. Box = three meals, priced at a flat $60, unchanged in three years; no substitutions, all-or-nothing (three meals or none).

## Cost facts (per box unless noted)
- Raw materials (ingredients): averaged ~$35/box, now ~$42/box — driven by waste and spoilage. Waste up ~30% over the last year. Because everything is "fresh," over-ordered ingredients must be destroyed.
- Kitchen labor (prepare + package): was $5/box, now $6.25/box — overtime and idle hours from unpredictable promotions, plus cramped conditions.
- Transportation (linehaul + local): was < $7/box before the four new cities opened; has roughly DOUBLED per box since.
- SG&A: ~5% of sales in SA/SF/SJ, ~7% in the newer/distant markets.

### Delivery arrangements (transportation detail)
- Sacramento area: outsourced courier TruckieCo loads at the kitchen and delivers door-to-door for $5.50/box, all-in.
- San Francisco & San Jose: contracted trucking firm KC Fish runs a full truck to a pool point in each city; boxes are then split into local vans for home delivery.
  - Truck: 53', up to 28 pallets, 32 boxes/pallet (4 layers x 8) = ~900 boxes/truck.
  - Linehaul (moves under 1000 miles): $150 per load + $1.75 per mile, independent of box count up to truck capacity.
  - Loading in Sacramento: ~$0.75/box.
  - Local van delivery from pool point to homes: ~$6/box, all-in.
- Four new cities (LA, SD, LV, PH): full-service carrier Ace Trucking loads at the Sacramento kitchen and charges a FLAT per-box fee by destination: $16 LA, $18 SD, $21 LV, $22 PH. Ace reports no capacity constraints.

## The directors and their (conflicting) views
- Jon Bush (CEO/founder): obsessed with GROWTH; "high costs don't worry me, poor service does." Wants new product lines (wine, ice cream), national expansion (NY, Boston, Chicago, Seattle), and rural/suburban reach.
- Dougie Walsh (Transportation): employee #2, knows delivery cold. Notes transportation cost per box has doubled. Suggests redesigning SF/SJ pool points and renegotiating Ace.
- Lisa Cross (Nutrition/menus): designs menus for freshness and variety, proud that in a typical week NO ingredients overlap between meals; resists modular/shared ingredients; complains sourcing is too slow. Forecasts each meal but won't explain how.
- Eddie White (Sourcing): says forecasting is the root problem — total boxes are stable but per-meal estimates are bad, causing ~30% more waste. Blames Lisa's forecasts and marketing's surprise promotions. Uses the trucking spot market for pickups; pays vendors on receipt. Raw-material cost $35 -> $42/box.
- Becky Schick (Kitchen Ops): kitchen is understaffed and over capacity; overtime from surprise promotions. Labor $5 -> $6.25/box. Suggests expanding Sacramento or opening a second kitchen (candidates: Fresno, Bakersfield).
- Linda Chin (Marketing): drove promotions that doubled sales in the new cities; wants a kitchen in every city, 12 menu options instead of 6, product substitutions, daily delivery, more promotions/discounts. Blames ops/sourcing/transportation for being inflexible.
