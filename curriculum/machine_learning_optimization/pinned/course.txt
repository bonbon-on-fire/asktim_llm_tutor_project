Course description

15.095 Machine Learning under a Modern Optimization Lens gives a unified, rigorous, and non-heuristic treatment of machine learning through three modern optimization lenses: convex, robust, and mixed-integer optimization. Where classical statistics starts from probability models, this course de-emphasizes them: it starts with data and revisits the central problems of ML — regression, classification, trees, clustering, matrix completion, and prescription — as formal optimization problems. The central claim, demonstrated throughout on real and synthetic datasets, is that modern optimization can find solutions to large-scale ML problems in seconds or minutes, certify them optimal in minutes or hours, and outperform classical heuristic approaches out of sample. The course develops robust and sparse linear regression, stable and holistic regression, median and convex regression, robust and sparse classification, optimal classification and regression trees, kernel methods, and prescriptive and policy trees, then turns to deep learning, multimodal and explainable multimodal AI, prescriptive deep learning, debiasing observational data for precision medicine, missing-data imputation, interpretable clustering, matrix completion, and interpretable optimization. Guiding principles run through the material: interpretability matters, the link between ML and optimization is a source of insight, robustness is often more valuable than optimality, and a method should be judged by its practical tractability rather than by worst-case complexity. Recitations cover computational implementation in Julia. The course is taught by Dimitris Bertsimas and follows the text Bertsimas & Dunn, Machine Learning under a Modern Optimization Lens (Dynamic Ideas Press, 2019).
