Course Content:
Artificial intelligence is transforming how organizations operate, compete, innovate, and succeed. This course provides a practical introduction to the foundations of modern AI, from predictive machine learning to generative and agentic AI systems. The learning objectives are threefold: understanding the technologies underlying modern AI; building AI-powered products with AI as a coding companion; and identifying and leading opportunities to create value through AI. Students develop a principled understanding of how modern AI systems learn from data, make predictions, perform tasks autonomously, and support decision-making, and they gain hands-on experience building complete solution pipelines from data to deployment in Python. The course follows a problem-driven approach built around business case studies spanning marketing, finance, healthcare, energy, transportation, supply chains, sports analytics, manufacturing, and digital platforms.

Modules (Semester at a glance):
- Introduction (Session 1): Let's build an AI agent!
- Supervised machine learning (Sessions 2–7): the core supervised learning toolkit with structured data — linear and logistic regression, regularization, tree-based methods, feature engineering, and model selection.
- Deep learning (Sessions 8–15): deep neural networks for rich representations from unstructured data — neural networks, transformers, computer vision, and multimodal models, with emphasis on the transformer architecture.
- The frontier of AI (Sessions 16–22): large language models, AI applications, and AI agents — how foundation models reason, use tools, retrieve knowledge, and interact with users; deploying AI responsibly at scale.

Use of Generative AI:
The use of generative AI is encouraged throughout the course for coding, debugging, analysis, ideation, and communication, so that students can devote more attention to higher-level managerial thinking: framing the right problem, making sound modeling choices, interpreting results, evaluating tradeoffs, and translating analysis into decisions. Students have access to Claude Code via the MIT Sloan Claude Enterprise account. Two course-specific AI tools are provided: an AI assistant that answers questions about course content, and an AI tutor that provides problem-solving guidance. AI may assist with the work, but responsibility for the correctness, interpretation, and quality of every submitted deliverable remains with its authors.
