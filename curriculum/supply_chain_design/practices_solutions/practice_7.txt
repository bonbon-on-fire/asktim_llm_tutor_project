Practice Problem 1: Procurement at SmallCo

Part 1 — Correct answer: 60
Explanation: Net income previous year: 10-6-2-1=1M. Net income if raw material is 10% cheaper: 10-0.9*6-2-1=1.6M.
      Percent increase: (1.6-1)/1=60%
      Note that a 10% reduction in purchase prices leads to a much larger -- 60% -- increase in net income!

Part 2 — Correct answer: Supplier 2
Explanation: Current COGS is 6M, so 17% means current supplier costs $1.02M per year.
        Note that all costs are cash flows - the cost of the safety stock is the annual cost of keeping the stock (it is not the investment). Since the investment financing is predetermined there is no need to do any investment analysis using discounted cash flows.
        Supplier 1 is 10% cheaper, so: 1.02*0.9=0.918M. With additional costs: 0.918+0.1+0.06=1.078M
        Supplier 2 is 10% cheaper than Supplier 1, so: 1.02*0.9*0.9=0.8262M. With additional costs: 0.8262+0.175=1.0012M
        So - what does this tell us? Supplier 2 is slightly cheaper when looking at total costs, as long as reliability does not become worse.

Part 3 — Correct answer: 1018800
Explanation: The cost reduction is 1.02M-1.0012M=18,800. Adding this to previous net income gives the result.

Part 4 — Correct answer: 1.9
Explanation: The income increases by 18,800. The percent increase is 18,800/1,000,000=0.0188 or 1.9%.
      Note that with the change to Supplier 2, you reduce purchase price by 19% but only reach a net income improvement of 1.9%!

Part 5 — Correct answer: Carrier III
Explanation: Cost per mile:
        Carrier I: 2
        Carrier II: 1+0.02*47=1.94
        Carrier III: 1.4+0.01*47=1.87
        Hence, Carrier III is best at an index of 47

Part 6 — Correct answer: 60
Explanation: We find this be solving two equations
        First set Carrier I and Carrier III equal in cost: 2=1.4+0.01*x, leading to index x=60
        Next set Carrier II and Carrier III equal in cost: 1+0.02*x=1.4+0.1*x, leading to index x=40
        Hence, it is optimal to contract with Carrier I if the index is above 60.

Part 7 — Correct answer: 8.4
Explanation: The new cost for transportation is: 1.87*500,000=935,000
        This is a reduction in cost by: 1.0M-0.935M=65,000
        Since we had selected Supplier 2 in Part 2, with total cost for 1.0012M, which is 18,800 cheaper than the baseline (1.02M)
        New net income then is: 1,083,800 (1,000,000 + 65,000 + 18,800), which is an improvement of 83,800/1,000,000=8.38%

Explanation: To solve this, create a spreadsheet model where you separate costs and capacities into to different matrices according to the image below. Follow the steps in the lesson, and remember to constrain the decision variables to integer values! This should give you a solution with an optimal cost of 2860!

Part 2 — Correct answer: 2890
Explanation: See - as we heard in the lesson, and as you may no from earlier in this course, adding these types of constraints will never lead to a solution with a lower cost!
      When you set it up in Excel, make sure that total demand for a trade-lane is less than or equal to ten and that the capacity for each trade-lane-carrier combination (as given in the table) are not violated.
      To add the constraint, add a new 3x3 matrix, where you insert 75% of total demand (i.e. 7.5 units) for each trade lane-carrier combination. In the solver, make sure that for each trade-lane-carrier combination, the decision is less than or equal to the value of this matrix. (you can also just type "7.5" into the solver if you do not want to create the matrix). Remember to make sure that only integer solutions are allowed, setting this as a constraint in the solver!

Practice Problem 5: Producer&Gambler

Part 1 — Correct answer: 16566
Explanation: This is a combinatorial auction! As you see in the data, there are many packages to collect bids on, even though the number of lanes are few. This means the spreadsheet models grows very quickly.

        To set this up, make sure that every package-supplier is a decision variable. There should be 45 decision variables. Following the steps of the lesson should give you the result.

Part 2 — Correct answer: 16568
Explanation: This is ensured by introducing an item-level constraint, stating that the maximum capacity for each item is demand-1. Note that it is not a system-constraint, but an item constraint. This means that you need to sum up, for each supplier, the amount sold of each item. This amount must be less than demand-1 for each supplier.
      As we see, this leads to a very small increase in price! So it may be worth paying this price to diversify and have at least two suppliers for every product.
