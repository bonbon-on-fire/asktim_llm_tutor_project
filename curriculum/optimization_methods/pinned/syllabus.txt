Course Contents and Objectives:
Optimization methods are central to prescriptive analytics across operations research, management science, and engineering. Thanks to progress in algorithms and hardware, large-scale problems can now be solved routinely, enabling decision-support systems in manufacturing, transportation, energy, finance, health care, machine learning, and more. The course covers linear, non-linear, and integer optimization and optimization under uncertainty, with four objectives:
- Modeling: translate practical decision problems into well-structured mathematical models.
- Theory: understand the mathematical and geometric structure of a formulation and its implications for scalability.
- Algorithms: the technology at the core of modern solvers, plus designing algorithms for problems off-the-shelf solvers cannot handle.
- Practice: model complex problems, build decision-support tools, and turn results into recommendations.
Lectures are complemented by computational implementation in Julia using JuMP.
