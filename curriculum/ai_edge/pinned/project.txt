Team project description

Overview:
The projects are opportunities to design, prototype, and pitch AI-powered solutions to real business and policy problems. The goal is to identify a meaningful problem, determine the role of AI to address it, build a solution, evaluate its performance and impact, and communicate its value. Projects are completed in self-formed teams of two. Teams are encouraged to explore problems connected to their professional experience, personal interests, entrepreneurial ideas, or societal challenges. A project may address a real organization, a fictional client grounded in a realistic setting, a potential startup, or a broader policy problem. The scope should be ambitious but focused — a problem that permits meaningful analysis and evaluation in a few weeks.

Each project should address four questions:
(i)   What problem are we solving? Define the target stakeholder, the current process or decision, and the opportunity for improvement.
(ii)  What can AI do? Acquire appropriate data and design an AI-powered solution.
(iii) Does it work? Evaluate the solution rigorously against appropriate benchmarks.
(iv)  Does it matter? Translate technical performance into managerial or societal impact (e.g., revenue, cost, profit, risk, quality).
Creativity is strongly encouraged, but complexity is not an objective in itself. A simple model or application that addresses an important problem and produces actionable value is more valuable than a technically elaborate system with unclear value.

Project 1 (November 20) — Predictive AI:
Develop a predictive AI solution to a business or policy problem. Teams may combine machine learning with tabular data and deep learning with unstructured data into a unified project, or pursue a more ambitious deep learning project using unstructured and potentially multimodal data. Teams acquire an appropriate dataset (public datasets, APIs, or permitted organizational data — no confidential, proprietary, or personally identifiable information for public portfolios), translate the problem into a well-defined prediction task, prepare the data, perform rigorous model selection and validation, and establish meaningful comparisons against relevant benchmarks (predictive baseline, business rule, alternative modeling approaches). Beyond technical performance, teams quantify how improved predictions translate into business or policy improvements. Sample topics: real estate valuation; employee attrition and workforce planning; insurance claims; hospitality demand and customer experience; predictive maintenance; retail and e-commerce; healthcare operations; manufacturing quality control; transportation and mobility; agricultural monitoring.

Project 2 (December 08) — Generative and agentic AI:
Design and prototype an agentic AI application built on LLMs, retrieval-augmented generation, or AI agents. Teams identify the knowledge, data, tools, and external systems the application requires and design an appropriate workflow; the resulting prototype should be functional and demonstrate the solution's capabilities. Teams make thoughtful choices about models, prompts, knowledge sources, tools, and agent behavior, and rigorously evaluate the system via sample inputs and outputs illustrating both successful outcomes and failure modes. Beyond technical performance, teams evaluate how workflow augmentation delivers practical impact, and the pathways toward implementation, adoption, and change management. An honest analysis of an imperfect system, supported by thoughtful diagnosis and clear recommendations, is more valuable than a technically impressive model with erratic behavior. Sample topics: investment intelligence; clinical documentation; email management; expense processing; real estate.

Deliverables (per project):
- Two-minute video: a self-contained demo or pitch addressed to the target stakeholder.
- Presentation (5–10 slides): the problem, the AI solution, business impact, and recommendations, addressed to the target stakeholder. Project 1 focuses on data, modeling approach, and key findings; Project 2 focuses on the agentic workflow.
- Technical repository: a GitHub repository with technical components and a one-page README, addressed to a data scientist or ML engineer in the target organization. Project 1 includes executable code and reproduction instructions; Project 2 includes the application code or exported workflow with system architecture, setup, and usage.
Teams are encouraged to publish their solutions in a professional online portfolio. A selection of projects is showcased during Demo Day on December 9.
