Graded Assignment 1: Financial Impact of SC Decisions (Tea4U)

Part 1 — Cash capital-equipment purchase (Jan 1, 2022): effect on the income
statement and balance sheet.
Correct answer: "Property, plant, equipment increases" AND "Current Assets
decrease".
Explanation: Cash (a current asset) is spent to acquire a long-term asset, so PP&E
increases and current assets decrease. Because the purchase is early in the year
the asset also depreciates within 2022, so depreciation (a cost) rises and the
long-term assets do NOT increase by as much as cash fell — consequently total
liabilities + shareholder equity DECREASE (so "Total Liabilities and Shareholder's
Equity increase" is wrong).

Part 2 — Inventory reduced $650, sales reduced $1800: effect on working-capital
turnover.
Correct answer: Working capital turnover will increase by around 7.9%.
Explanation: WC turnover = Sales / Working Capital = Sales / (current assets −
current liabilities). Before: 52,779 / 6,181 = 8.54. After the project:
(52,779 − 1,800) / (6,181 − 650) = 50,979 / 5,531 = 9.22. Difference 9.22 − 8.54 =
0.68 ≈ a 7.94% increase. (A leaner firm — but whether it's worth the lost sales is
a strategic call.)

Part 3 — Cash-to-cash cycle after the inventory-reduction project.
Correct answer: 20.6 days.
Explanation: CCC = DIO + DSO − DPO. After the project the average inventory and
sales change: new average inventory = avg(4,289, 4,289 − 650) = 3,964; new sales =
52,779 − 1,800 = 50,979 (COGS, AR, AP unchanged from 2021). CCC =
(Avg Inv / COGS)·365 + (Avg AR / Sales)·365 − (Avg AP / COGS)·365 = 20.6.

Part 4 — Which actions DEFINITELY reduce the cash-to-cash cycle?
Correct answer: "Transferring ownership of incoming supplies at the shipment
destination instead of at the shipment point of origin" AND "Reducing the
production time in a Make-To-Order setting and receiving payments from customers
at the time of order instead of at the time of delivery".
Explanation: Actions that reduce inventory, accelerate receipts from customers, or
delay payments to suppliers all lower CCC. Taking ownership later (at destination)
shortens the time inventory sits on your books; MTO with payment at order time
cuts both production time and DSO. The other options increase inventory or have
ambiguous/opposing effects (e.g. paying suppliers faster or giving customers more
time raises CCC).

Graded Assignment 2: ABC Costing (Leche fresca)
(Activity-Based Costing: allocate weekly resource costs to activities by usage %,
compute cost-driver rates, then cost-to-serve for new zones.)

[Captured instance — weekly costs: personnel (sales rep) order processing 760;
other-than-personnel order processing 180; personnel (drivers/helpers) delivery
1180; fuel & operating delivery 1030.]

Part 1 — Total cost allocated to two activities.
Correct answer: Order processing (traditional channel) = 716; Delivery (modern
channel) = 545.
Explanation: 80% of sales reps' time and 60% of equipment hours go to the
traditional channel: 760·0.8 + 180·0.6 = 716. 20% of drivers' time and 30% of fuel
go to modern delivery: 1180·0.2 + 1030·0.3 = 545.

Part 2 — Cost-driver rates (note: DIFFERENT activities than Part 1).
Correct answer: Order processing (modern channel) = 0.64; Delivery (traditional
channel) = 33.3.
Explanation: Order processing for the modern channel totals $224 and there are 350
modern orders => 224 / 350 = 0.64 per order. Delivery for the traditional channel
totals $1,665 and serves 50 traditional customers => 1,665 / 50 = 33.3 per
customer.

Part 3 — Cost to serve two new city zones.
Correct answer: Zone A = 706; Zone B = 1186 (1186.02 before rounding).
Explanation: Using the four rates (order proc traditional 2.86, order proc modern
0.64, delivery traditional 33.3, delivery modern 30.28):
Zone A: 2.86·20 + 0.64·20 + 33.3·10 + 30.28·10 = 706.0.
Zone B: 2.86·12 + 0.64·12 + 33.3·18 + 30.28·18 = 1186.02.

Graded Assignment 3: Cash Cycle (Technograph)
(Cash conversion cycle from financial-statement figures.)

[Captured instance — Starting inventory 11336, Ending inventory 1248; Starting AR
7451, Ending AR 6636; Starting AP 4986, Ending AP 5183; COGS 109097; Sales
134534. All in thousands of dollars.]

Part 1 — Averages (average of starting and ending values).
Correct answer: Average Inventory = 6292; Average Accounts Receivable = 7043.5;
Average Accounts Payable = 5084.5.

Part 2 — Days ratios.
Correct answer: Days of Inventory Outstanding (DIO) = 21; Days of Payables
Outstanding (DPO) = 17; Days of Sales Outstanding (DSO) = 19.
Explanation: DIO = Average Inventory / COGS · 365; DPO = Average Accounts Payable /
COGS · 365; DSO = Average Accounts Receivable / Sales · 365.

Part 3 — Cash-to-cash (cash conversion) cycle.
Correct answer: 23 days.
Explanation: CCC = DIO − DPO + DSO = 21 − 17 + 19 = 23.
