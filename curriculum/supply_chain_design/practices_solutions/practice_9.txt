Practice Problem 1: MacKenzie Markets

Part 1 — Correct answer: Equipment sale after three years at book value; Equipment purchase of 2,000,000; Floor manager and maintenance staff salaries of 300,000
Explanation: The equipment purchase is clearly relevant: cash is flowing out of Mackenzie with the initiative and it would not without the initiative.
        Similarly, the equipment sale after three years at book value is relevant since it is planned to occur with the initiative and there would be no proceeds without it.
        The warehouse labor cost is different with or without the initiative, and cash is flowing for these paychecks.
        The lease of forklifts and other material handling equipment was planned; since it is now foregone, this opportunity cost is relevant. The key is that it was planned to occur and with the robots it would not.
        It says clearly that the implementation requires a new floor manager and maintenance staff, so it is different with or without and is relevant.
        The warehouse had already been leased at 900,000 per year. Even though the lease is an annual cost, it is a binding agreement for the term. Thus, it is a sunk cost and is not relevant.
        The warehouse manager has clearly been hired already and thus his salary would not change with or without the equipment.
        The transportation cost reduction is tricky. The presence of the warehouse enables this to occur, so it seems that the savings would occur with or without the robots. With no further information about how the robots enable better transportation, it is safe to assume this is not relevant for this initiative. Transportation savings were likely incorporated already in the decision to lease the warehouse and we do not want to count them again.
        Cash is flowing for the technical staff salaries, but it would occur with or without this initiative. Since they appear to be idle after the last implementation, there is no opportunity cost with reassigning them. Hence, these salaries add no incremental cost for this initiative.
        The training cost for robot control systems happened already and is a sunk cost.
        This information should help you calculate the free cash flows.

Part 2 — Correct answer: -2000 | 410 | 410 | 1810
Explanation: The previous question on relevant cash flows should have helped you calculate the NOPAT as long as you applied depreciation and calculated taxes correctly. Straight-line depreciation over 10 years results in 200,000 per year. You need to add the depreciation back after the tax calculation since it is not a relevant cash flow. For example, the calculation for Period 1 would be:
      EBITDA 500 (net of labor savings, material handling lease foregone, manager/staff salaries added)
      EBIT 300 (after depreciation of 200)
      NOPAT 210 (after taxes of 90)
      Free Cash Flow 410 (adding back the depreciation of 200)
      The purchase price of 2,000,000 occurs immediately and thus is the initial Net Capital Expenditure in period 0. After three years, the book value is 2,000,000-(200,000*3)=1,400,000. Mackenzie assumes it will sell the equipment for that book value; thus, this salvage value income requires no tax adjustment. Note that the salvage value is entered as negative because it is income, not expenditure. When subtracted from the FCF equation, this salvage value becomes a positive cash flow (inflow to Mackenzie). Both the purchase price and salvage value are relevant CapEx.
      If you did not come up with these free cash flows, then use these answers for calculating the figures of merit:
      Initial -2000
      Year 1 410
      Year 2 410
      Year 3 1810

Part 3 — Correct answer: 71
Explanation: The answer to the previous question gave Free Cash Flows through Year 3 of -2000, 410, 410, 1810. The NPV is calculated using the Excel formula: NPV(0.1, 410, 410, 1810) – 2000 = 71.45. With the positive NPV at their discount rate, Mackenzie should invest in the robots.

Part 4 — Correct answer: 11.6
Explanation: To answer this question you calculate IRR of the free cash flows initially and through year three: -2000, 410, 410, 1810. The IRR is 11.57%, which rounds to 11.6% for this question. Any discount rate lower than that would yield a positive NPV. The limited range of discount rates for which this initiative would be worthwhile indicates that it is a good, but not great opportunity. Any risks or poor estimates in projected cash flows could undermine its success.

Part 5 — Correct answer: 2.65
Explanation: After two years the remaining investment to be paid back is 2000-410-410=1180. The free cash flow for year 3 is 1810. Thus, after 1180/1810 =0.652 of the final year the investment will be paid off. Adding this partial amount of year three to the previous two years of cash flows yields a payback period = 2+0.652=2.652, which rounds to 2.65 years.

Practice Problem 2: Madeira Importers

Part 1 — Correct answer: 300
Explanation: The net change in working capital = net increase in Accounts Receivable + net increase in Inventory – net increase in Accounts Payable. In period 0, this is 500,000 – 200,000 = 300,000.
        For subsequent working capital cash flows, we need to consider the net change from the previous period. In this case it is the difference between period 1 and initial requirements. For period 1, the calculations for each component of working capital are:
        Accounts Receivable 200,000 – 0 = 200,000.
        Inventory 700,000 – 500,000 = 200,000.
        Accounts Payable 300,000 – 200,000 = 100,000.
        The net change in working capital = net increase in Accounts Receivable + net increase in Inventory – net increase in Accounts Payable. The calculation is 200,000 + 200,000 – 100,000 = 300,000.

Part 2 — Correct answer: 300 | 700 | 0
Explanation: The calculations for these values are similar to the calculation of net change in working capital for Year 1. The table below shows the calculation of net change in each component as well as the working capital overall. Note that the working capital required in Period 4+ is 0. Since the perpetuity cash flows are intended to be stable, this is appropriate. All the values for the perpetuity match those in period 3.

      The table also shows the incremental income statement and the free cash flows for each period. If you did not calculate the same numbers, then use these instead to complete the exercise.

Part 3 — Correct answer: 7200
Explanation: The free cash flow for period 4+ is 720 and the discount rate is 10%. Using the formula for calculating the value of a perpetuity yields 720/0.10 = 7200.
        To incorporate the perpetuity value into the DCF analysis, add it to the period 3 free cash flow: 20 + 7200 = 7220. That results in a series of cash flows that consider the long term value of the private label products:
        Cash flows including perpetuity from year 0 through year 3: -300, -180, 0, 7220

Part 4 — Correct answer: 4961
Explanation: The answer to the previous question gave Free Cash Flows including the terminal value in period 3: -300, -180, 0, 7220. The NPV is calculated using the Excel formula: NPV(0.1,-180,0,7220)-300= 4960.9. Even though it takes a few years to generate positive free cash flows, the perpetuity of NOPAT makes the private label products a very worthwhile endeavor. Now you can also understand why retailers try hard to launch private label products with higher gross margins.

Practice Problem 4: More Practice on Discounting

Part 1 — Correct answer: 1050 | 1103 | 1158
Explanation: At the end of 2017, cash flows will be $1000 (given). This amount will increase by 5% over the next 3 years. In year 2018, cash flows will be ($1,000)*(1+.05). At the end of 2019, cash flow will be ($1,000)*(1+.05)^2. At the end of 2020, cash flow will be ($1,000)(1+.05)^3.

Part 2 — Correct answer: 1192 | 24562
Explanation: CF for 2021 is expected to grow 3% from 2020 cash flows. Thus, 2021 cash flows are expected to be $1157.63*(1+.03) = $1192.35
      2022 cash flows are expected to be $1157.63*(1+.03)^2 = $1228.12
      The value of future cash flows, after 2021, in terms of 2021 dollars is $1228.12/(.08-.03).

Part 3 — Correct answer: 21766.98
Explanation: To calculate the present value of each year's cash flows:
        2018 cash flows are discounted one year - $1050/(1+.08)^1 = $972.22
        2019 cash flows are discounted two years - $1102.5/(1+.08)^2 = $945.22
        2020 cash flows are discounted three years - $1157.63/(1+.08)^3 = $918.96
        2021 cash flows ($1192) and the 2021-value of all subsequent cash flows ($24562.49) are both discounted four years - (1192.35+24562.49)/(1+.08)^4 = $18,930.58.
        The net present value is the sum of the present values for all of these cash flows.
