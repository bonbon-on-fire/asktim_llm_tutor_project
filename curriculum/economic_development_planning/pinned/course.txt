Course description

This course explores how local and state governments use planning tools to attract, retain, and grow sustainable economic development. It surveys how government is funded in the United States, the development strategies that localities pursue, and how those efforts should be evaluated through multiple lenses — effectiveness, equity, and sustainability. The emphasis is on applied theory at the local level: rather than treating economic development as an abstract macroeconomic question, the course puts the aspiring planner into the position of a local practitioner who must decide whether, when, and how to intervene in the economy, and with which fiscal and regulatory instruments.

The course is organized around four units. LENSES asks why and how to interfere in the economy, ranging from Jane Jacobs' minimalist, import-replacement view to creative-class placemaking to equity- and social-justice-centered frameworks. TOOLS examines how local government actually works — property and sales taxes, intergovernmental transfers, annual budgeting and procurement, capital improvement programs, tax increment finance (TIF) districts, business improvement districts (BIDs), Opportunity Zones, zoning, and fiscal impact studies. STRATEGIES moves from concepts to action — determining local advantages and disadvantages, "buy local" models, incentives, value capture, infrastructure and public investment, and managing risk for businesses. CASES applies the material to real projects such as Thompson's Point in Portland, Maine, and Assembly Square in Somerville, Massachusetts.

The course is geared toward practice: students write short professional memoranda in the role of a city's Deputy Mayor for Planning & Economic Development, a midterm paper examining a single lens or tool, and a final case-study project that requires direct community engagement (not online research alone) and evaluates a real economic development effort through both holistic and financial frameworks. This is MIT course 11.438, taught by Jeffrey Levine, AICP, in the Department of Urban Studies and Planning (Graduate, Spring 2020).
