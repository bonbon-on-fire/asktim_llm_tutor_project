### Weekly report — what it shows and how it works

The report has four parts, top to bottom:

- **Live stats.** Conversations, messages, students, new students, and cost, with week-over-week arrows. Computed **live from the database** on each page load (no LLM call). The **"Cost" here is the tutoring cost** — students' own token spend during conversations, summed from usage rows — _not_ our analysis cost.
- **Daily activity.** Messages per day, Sunday–Saturday, also live from the database.
- **AI review.** One short narrative paragraph **per course** summarizing the week's major questions and themes and how the tutoring went overall. It's the qualitative headline — read this instead of every transcript. Scoped per course, so a course login sees only its own review.
- **Conversation flags (master-only).** Conversations the judge scored **below the pass threshold (32 / 40)** — i.e. where the tutor worked under 80% performance. This is internal-only (master login); the server withholds it entirely from course-scoped logins.

**Backend — how it's produced:**

- An **offline weekly job** judges every conversation in the week against the **40-point rubric_08 grader** (`claude-sonnet-4-6`, temperature 0), extracts the week's popular topics (a cheap **Haiku 4.5** call), synthesizes the per-course AI review (one **Haiku 4.5** call per course), and writes a **committed JSON cache file per week**.
- The Dashboard just **reads that committed cache**, scopes it to the login's course(s), and renders the cards. No expensive judging happens on page load — it's all precomputed, so the report is instant and free to view.
- **Reuse keeps re-runs cheap:** each conversation's transcript is hashed; on a re-run, unchanged conversations reuse their prior verdict and cost **$0**. Only new or edited conversations get re-judged.

**Added cost (our analysis cost — separate from the tutoring cost above):**

- The **judge** (`claude-sonnet-4-6`, $3 in / $15 out per 1M tokens) is essentially the entire bill. The **topics** and **per-course AI review** calls are cheap **Haiku 4.5** and negligible next to it.
- Cost tracks **transcript length, not conversation count**. A typical conversation runs **~$0.013–0.015** all-in (that's the median), but a single very long transcript can cost far more — in the busy week below, **one outlier conversation alone cost $2.34**.
- **Actual measured cost per week** (from the committed caches, not an estimate):

  | Week (Sun–Sat ET) | Conversations judged | Analysis cost |
  | ----------------- | -------------------- | ------------- |
  | Jul 12–18         | 1                    | $0.02         |
  | Jul 19–25         | 0                    | $0.00         |
  | Jul 26–Aug 1      | 4                    | $0.06         |
  | Aug 2–8           | 28                   | $0.37         |
  | Aug 9–15          | 88                   | **$3.96**     |

- So the busy 88-conversation week actually ran **$3.96** — higher than volume alone predicts (~$1.6), almost entirely because of that one **$2.34** outlier transcript. Take the outlier out and a normal busy week lands around **$1–2**; quiet weeks stay **under $0.10**.
- **Takeaway for cost:** the risk isn't volume, it's the occasional pathological long conversation. One runaway transcript can dominate a week's bill, so it's worth watching per-conversation cost, not just the weekly total.
- Backfilling everything judged so far — **121 conversations across 5 weeks — cost about $4.40 total** (and re-running any of it is free for unchanged conversations, per the hash reuse above).

**How it runs today (and the plan to automate):**

- **Right now it's manual.** The automatic weekly schedule is **intentionally disabled** — we run each completed week by hand, one at a time, and check the cost before running the next. There is no scheduled job firing yet.
- **The plan** is a scheduled job on **Sunday ~08:00 UTC** (just after the Sun–Sat week closes at Saturday midnight ET) that judges the completed week and publishes its cache with no manual step. That autodeploy is spec'd but stays off until we're comfortable letting it bill unattended.

### Flagged visibility — show to course staff, or keep internal?

Current state: the **AI review is already shown to course staff** (scoped to their own course), while the **Flags (raw pass/fail judgments) stay internal** (master-only). The open question is whether that split is right.

**Arguments for showing it to everyone:**

- **Transparency.** Staff see the same QA we do; nothing is hidden, which builds trust.
- **Fast qualitative read.** Staff get a sense of what students asked and where the tutor struggled without reading every transcript.
- **Early problem-surfacing.** Staff can catch and flag issues we'd otherwise miss.

**Arguments for keeping (parts of) it internal:**

- **Staff don't know the rubric.** A score like 28/40 or the pass threshold of 32 is meaningless without the rubric behind it — staff may read a "fail" as far worse than it is.
- **The judge isn't always right.** It can misgrade or misunderstand a conversation (unusual pedagogy, missing context, a student being sarcastic). A wrong flag shown to staff could alarm them or erode confidence in the tool.
- **Noise / concern.** Staff may fixate on individual flagged conversations instead of trends, or worry over an auto-generated critique of their course's tutor.

**Middle ground (what we've built):** narrative **AI review** to staff, raw **Flags/scores internal**. If we do surface scores later, pair them with a short rubric explainer and an explicit caveat that the judge is an imperfect, automated first pass — not a verdict.
