THE SITUATION
You are a recent hire on the commercial team at Ardent Materials, a ready-mix
concrete producer with 11 plants across the (fictional) Meridian region. Ardent
supplies about 900,000 m3 of concrete a year to commercial builders and public
authorities, on margins that have not exceeded 7% in a decade.

Cement is what makes concrete hard, and it is also what makes it expensive and
dirty: producing a ton of Portland cement releases close to a ton of CO2, and
cement is roughly 8% of global emissions. Everything else in the mix — water,
sand, gravel — is cheap and carbon-free. Two industrial by-products can replace
part of the cement: ground granulated blast-furnace slag (from steelmaking) and
fly ash (from coal combustion). Both are cheaper and lower-carbon, but they react
more slowly, so a heavily blended concrete can be disappointing at 7 days and
adequate at 28. Over the years Ardent has run about a thousand trial batches in a
lab; that archive is your data.

THE ARCHIVE (mix_archive.csv, 1,003 rows; split into mix_archive_train.csv = 765
rows and mix_archive_test.csv = 238 rows). Each row is one cylinder. All ingredient
quantities are kilograms per cubic metre (kg/m3).
- batch_id: identifier of the trial batch
- cement: Portland cement — the binder, and the source of nearly all the carbon
- slag, fly_ash: cement substitutes, both industrial by-products
- water: mixing water
- plasticizer: chemical admixture that keeps a low-water mix workable
- coarse_agg, fine_agg: gravel and sand
- age_days: days of curing before the cylinder was tested (only eight distinct
  values in the whole archive — the lab tests on a fixed schedule)
- strength_mpa: measured 28-day-style compressive strength in MPa — the TARGET

The seven "ingredient" columns used as core features:
  core_features = [cement, slag, fly_ash, water, plasticizer, coarse_agg, fine_agg]

EXPLORATORY FINDINGS (Section 1). Historic mixes span the full range, from pure
cement to mixes where more than two-thirds of the binder is a by-product. Strength
rises steeply between 3 and 28 days and then flattens — the gain from 3 to 28 days is
far larger than from 90 to 365. That flattening is the reason treating age as a
straight-line continuous variable is suspect and Task 1 exists.

THE MODELS (Sections 2-3), all ordinary least squares via statsmodels, reported with
a helper (print_summary_with_stars) that prints coefficients, significance stars, R2,
and adjusted R2:
- Model 0 (historical baseline): strength ~ cement only. Train R2 about 0.25 — more
  cement means more strength, but it cannot tell a good blend from a bad one.
- Model 1 (the new approach): strength ~ seven ingredients + age_days (age as a plain
  number). Out-of-sample R2 about 0.59. Slag and fly ash both carry positive,
  significant coefficients; water is negative. On the test set: Test R2 ~ 0.59,
  Test MAE ~ 8.1, Test RMSE ~ 10.1 MPa.
- Accuracy AT 28 DAYS is what matters, because the tender is decided on 28-day
  strength. Isolating the 107 test batches cured to 28 days, Model 1's RMSE at 28 days
  is about 8.41 MPa and it predicts LOW by about 3.0 MPa on average. That 8.41 is
  stored as rmse_28_m1 and drives the bid buffer.

FROM PREDICTIONS TO A BID (Section 4). Tender constants encoded in the notebook:
  PROJECT_VOLUME = 45,000 m3;  SPEC_STRENGTH = 30 MPa (required at 28 days);
  CARBON_CAP = 250 kg CO2e/m3;  RELIABILITY_K = 1.65 (aim 1.65 SD high -> ~5% risk
  of falling short);  BATCHING_COST = 48.0 $/m3.
  CO2 factors (kg CO2e per kg): cement 0.900, slag 0.080, fly_ash 0.015, water 0.0003,
  plasticizer 1.900, coarse_agg 0.007, fine_agg 0.007.
  Prices ($ per kg): cement 0.135, slag 0.115, fly_ash 0.065, water 0.0015,
  plasticizer 1.200, coarse_agg 0.017, fine_agg 0.021.
Carbon per m3 and cost per m3 are each the sum over ingredients of quantity x factor
(cost also adds the $48/m3 batching cost). The buffer logic: the model predicts the
AVERAGE strength of a mix, so a mix designed for exactly 30 MPa would fail on roughly
half of pours; to hold the shortfall risk near 5%, aim 1.65 RMSE above spec, i.e.
  target strength = 30 + 1.65 x (28-day RMSE).
With Model 1's 8.41 MPa that is 43.9 MPa.

THE SEVEN CANDIDATE MIXES (candidate_mixes.csv), all to be tested at age_days = 28:
  M1 Ardent Standard C30 (current) — 0% SCM, 378 kg CO2e/m3, $139.71/m3 (fails carbon cap)
  M2 Blend 31 — 31% SCM, 298 kg CO2e/m3, $143.73/m3 (fails carbon cap)
  M3 Ternary 54 — 54% SCM, 246 kg CO2e/m3, $148.54/m3
  M4 Ternary 48 — 48% SCM, 239 kg CO2e/m3, $141.33/m3
  M5 Ternary 43 — 43% SCM, 228 kg CO2e/m3, $135.16/m3
  M6 Fly Ash 45 — 45% SCM, 213 kg CO2e/m3, $129.96/m3
  M7 High Slag 58 — 58% SCM, 246 kg CO2e/m3, $155.26/m3
(SCM = supplementary cementitious material = slag + fly ash as a share of binder.)
Priya's Model 1 screen (target 43.9 MPa + 250 cap) clears only M7, so her preliminary
recommendation is to bid M7 at $155.26/m3 — the most expensive option.

HELPER FUNCTIONS PROVIDED IN THE NOTEBOOK (so you do not rebuild them)
- print_summary_with_stars(X_train, y_train): fits sm.OLS with an added constant and
  prints the coefficient table with significance stars; returns (model, coef_table).
- predict_with(model, X): predicts after adding the constant column.
- evaluate_model(name, y_tr, y_tr_pred, y_te, y_te_pred): returns Train_R2, Test_R2,
  Test_MAE, Test_RMSE.
- rmse_at_28(preds) and mean_error_at_28(preds): error on the 28-day test batches only.
- encode_age(frame): one-hot encodes curing age against AGE_LEVELS and lines the columns
  up with X_train2.
