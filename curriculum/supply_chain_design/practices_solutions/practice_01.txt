Practice Problem 1: Network Model Basics

Part 1 — Correct answer: x_1A
Explanation: The index tells you which two nodes the arc connects. In this course
we consider only unidirectional arcs, so the index also gives the direction of
flow. If the index is "1B" the flow goes from plant 1 to distribution center B.
(Arc from P1 to CA is therefore x_1A.)

Part 2 — Correct answer: For all nodes i, the sum of all outgoing flows from node
i must be less than or equal to the supply capacity of node i.
Explanation: Read the constraint sum_j x_ij <= S_i, for all i in S from the back.
"for all i in S" means it applies to every node i in the set S of Supply nodes
(the three plants in Part 1's figure). The x_ij are flows from node i to node j,
measured in units moved. Summing over j (the receiving nodes: DCs and customer
regions) counts all units transported out of node i. So for every supply node,
the sum of all outgoing flows must be <= the available supply S_i at that node.

Practice Problem 2: SteelCo

Part 1 — Correct answer: 100000  (total pounds shipped from Allentown DC)
Explanation: A transportation problem with two supply nodes and four demand nodes;
supply nodes have capacity constraints, demand nodes have demand constraints.
Use the transportation costs as the objective-function coefficients on the
decision variables, build the model in a spreadsheet, and use the Simplex solver
to find the cost-minimizing allocation. Summing all shipments out of Allentown
gives 100,000 (its full capacity).

Part 2 — Correct answer: 4569920  (minimum total cost)
Explanation: Same transportation model as Part 1; the minimum cost is the value
of the objective-function cell in the optimal (Simplex) solution.

Part 3 — Correct answer: Toledo, OH  (the DC with additional available capacity)
Explanation: Looking at the LHS vs RHS of the capacity constraints, only
Allentown's LHS equals its RHS — Allentown's capacity is fully utilized (a
binding / active constraint). Toledo's is not, so Toledo has spare capacity.

Practice Problem 3: Ralph Calvin Hilfiger (RCH) Industries

Part 1 — Correct answer: 380  (totes shipped through crossdock XD2 per week)
Explanation: A transshipment problem with five supply nodes, two transshipment
nodes, and five demand nodes. Build a spreadsheet model with supply and demand
constraints as in a transportation problem, plus a flow-balance constraint at the
transshipment (crossdock) nodes: incoming flow minus outgoing flow = 0. Ensure
the objective function includes all decision variables. The optimum ships 380
totes/week through crossdock XD2.

Part 2 — Correct answer: DC 4; DC 5
Explanation: In the optimal solution, DCs 1–3 are supported by the existing
crossdock XD1, while DCs 4 and 5 are supported by the new crossdock XD2 (200 and
180 totes respectively).

Part 3 — Correct answer: 30220  (minimum cost, $)
Explanation: The minimum cost is the final value of the target/objective cell.

Part 4 — Correct answer: All of the above
Explanation: None of the listed changes affects the optimal solution:
(1) a 400-tote weekly cap per crossdock — the current flow is <= 380 anyway;
(2) closing Factory 5 — it is unused in the optimum;
(3) reducing Factory 1's capacity to 190 — it only supplies 190 in the optimum.

Practice Problem 4: SandyCo — Multiple Optima

Part 1 — Correct answer: 69200  (minimum weekly cost with the suggested flows)
Explanation: The suggested flows give the same $69,200 cost — this problem has
"multiple optima" (more than one set of decisions achieving the lowest cost).
Common in small problems, rarer as complexity grows. The lesson's solution is one
optimum (what most Excel solvers return); another solver may return a different
optimal solution, since the algorithm only targets lowest cost, not a specific
set of decisions.

Part 2 — Correct answer: 100  (tons Plant 2 -> DC B)
Explanation: With the new costs it is cheaper to deliver via DC A. Plant 1 supplies
DC A at lowest cost and maxes its capacity; Plant 2 delivers the remaining demand
(100 tons) via DC B. (Re-solving gives total cost $68,325.)

Part 3 — Correct answer: 72  (tons DC A -> Region 3)
Explanation: Counterintuitively, even with higher demand, less is shipped DC A ->
Region 3. Plant 1's capacity is maxed, so the entire demand increase is handled by
Plant 2; the Region 3 increase is met via the more expensive DC B route
(205+145 vs 180+165), and the Region 1 increase is handled by Plant 1 via DC A
(supplying Region 1 via DC B is too expensive).

Part 4 — Correct answer: 25  (tons Plant 2 -> Region 1 directly)
Explanation: With the on-site machine, all of Region 1's demand is fulfilled by
Plant 2 directly at lower cost than via the DCs (275 vs 190+175=365). Plant 2
ships 25 directly to Region 1; Regions 2 and 3 are supplied via the DCs. Add the
direct-transport link as an 11th decision variable in the supply/demand
constraints. Optimal cost = $66,300.

Part 5 — Correct answer: 95  (tons Plant 1 should produce)
Explanation: Add a total-capacity constraint on DC B (sum its incoming flow;
conservation of flow keeps incoming = outgoing). This forces shipments toward
DC A. Since Plant 1 can supply DC A far more cheaply than Plant 2, more production
shifts back to Plant 1. Neither plant runs at full capacity.

Practice Problem 5: Food Delivery Startup

Part 1 — Correct answer: 36  (weighted X-coordinate)
Explanation: Weighted average of the nodes' x-coordinates, weighted by each node's
share of total weight:
x = (250/500)*20 + (150/500)*20 + (100/500)*100 = 36.

Part 2 — Correct answer: 57  (weighted Y-coordinate)
Explanation: Same logic for y:
y = (250/500)*30 + (150/500)*40 + (100/500)*150 = 57.

Part 3 — Correct answer: 45.27  (avg weighted distance, center-of-gravity)
Explanation: The center-of-gravity method uses the weighted coordinates from
Parts 1–2 as the business location. Sum the weighted distances from that location
to the three nodes and divide by the total weight.

Part 4 — Correct answer: 31.84  (avg weighted distance, Weber)
Explanation: Smaller than the center-of-gravity result because the Weber method
explicitly minimizes distance. Set up a small optimization with the business x/y
as decision variables and total weighted distance as the objective; use the
non-linear GRG solver with upper bounds (e.g. 150). Optimal coordinates (20,30),
total weighted distance 15,922; divide by total weight for the average (31.84).

Practice Problem 6: Temporary Networks

Part 1 — Correct answer: DC 5
Explanation: A facility-location problem: flows from a DC (supply) to shelters
(demand). Model it like the NERD model in a spreadsheet. "Transport work" (tonkm)
= distance x units, so distance acts as a variable transport cost per unit; use
the outbound distances as the outbound costs. This is a one-stage model — inbound
(military-controlled) costs are ignored. Include the linking constraints. To force
a single DC, constrain the sum of the binary y-variables to <= 1 (Pmin=Pmax=1).
Optimal single DC is DC 5. (Objective 580,910 = 480,910 kit-distance + 100,000
fixed cost.)

Part 2 — Correct answer: 4  (optimal number of DCs)
Explanation: With expensive outbound transport ($2/kit-mile), the model opens more
DCs to cut outbound cost (assuming network-management costs are in the fixed cost).
Set outbound cost = $2 x distance in the objective and relax the DC-count
constraint (sum of y <= Pmax=5). Optimum is 4 DCs; total cost $713,420.

Part 3 — Correct answer: 2  (optimal number of DCs when fixed cost = $300k)
Explanation: Just change the fixed cost in the model. The optimum is sensitive to
it — a higher fixed cost relative to transport cost yields fewer DCs. Optimum is
2 DCs; total cost $1,165,660.

Part 4 — Correct answer: It is optimal to open DC 2 and DC 5
Explanation: Adding the "at least 60% of demand within 2 miles" service constraint
changes the optimal locations (higher cost $1,259,780, better service). Build a
matrix flagging arcs under 2 miles; require sumproduct(that matrix, decision
variables)/demand >= 60%. This yields DC 2 and DC 5. Note: you must make the
demand constraints binding (equality in solver), otherwise the model over-ships
kits (e.g. to shelter 6) and the service constraint isn't truly satisfied.
