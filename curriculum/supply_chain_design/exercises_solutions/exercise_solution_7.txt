TITLE: Week 7 Graded Assignment — Answer Key & Explanations

graded assignments (maps to exercises/exercise_7.txt: GA1 Boston Watercolor

⚠ PARAMETRIZATION NOTE (important):
The graded assignments are randomized per student — the input tables (demands,
distances, unit counts, capacities, prices) and even the exact multiple-choice
option wording differ between variants. The numbers below are from ONE captured

their exercises/exercise_7.txt. Treat the categorical/framework answers and the

Graded Assignment 1: Boston Watercolor Academy
(Kraljic purchasing portfolio — classify each spend by total spend vs. supply
risk, then recommend the matching sourcing strategy.)

Part 1 — Sketching supplies
Correct answer (category): Tactical
Correct answer (recommendations, this variant): "The Academy could consider using
a procurement card to buy sketching supplies." AND "The Academy should try to
minimize the number of transactions related to sketching supplies."
Explanation: Low relative spend and low risk (cheap, standard commodities, many
substitutable suppliers with excess capacity) => a TACTICAL buy. For tactical
items the goal is to cut transaction cost/effort: procurement cards, fewer
transactions, streamlined acquisition — not supplier development or strategic
alliances.

Part 2 — Watercolor paper
Correct answer (category): Leverage
Correct answer (recommendations, this variant): "The Academy should retain
flexibility in supply, monitor alternatives and be ready to switch suppliers."
AND "The Academy should consider leveraging the volume of watercolor paper they
buy, e.g. by buying together with other schools." AND "The Academy should assure
supply continuity for watercolor paper."
Explanation: High relative spend but low risk (standard spec, several suppliers
with excess capacity, easy to substitute brands) => a LEVERAGE buy. Use buying
power (consolidate volume), keep alternatives open and switch as needed.

Part 3 — Watercolor brushes
Correct answer (category): Critical
Correct answer (recommendations, this variant): "They should consider using
brushes without the logo of the Academy. These will perform just as well, cost
less money and make the Academy less dependent on a few suppliers." AND "The
Academy may want to help its students to take good care of the brushes, so that
they last a long time." AND "The Academy may want to keep healthy level of safety
stock of brushes, to ensure continuity of supply in case of disruption."
Explanation: Low relative spend but high risk (custom-engraved specialty item,
few capable suppliers, long lead times) => a CRITICAL buy. Reduce dependence and
protect supply: de-specify (drop the logo), extend item life, hold safety stock.

Part 4 — Paint tubes and pans
Correct answer (category): Strategic
Correct answer (recommendation, this variant): "The Academy could consider a
strategic alliance with suppliers of watercolor paints."
Explanation: High relative spend and high risk (largest fraction of spend, no
substitute for high quality, few suppliers with limited capacity and stockouts)
=> a STRATEGIC buy. Manage through close, long-term partnership/alliance with key
suppliers.

Graded Assignment 2: Shiny Star Clothing
(Transportation / carrier-auction optimization: assign TEU freight from four
plants — Chongqing, Beijing, Nanjing, Shijiazhuang — to carriers Red/Blue/Green
to minimize total cost = sum over lanes of bid × distance × TEUs.)

[Captured instance — weekly demand: Chongqing 15, Beijing 20, Nanjing 26,
Shijiazhuang 13. Distances (km): 1750 / 1400 / 450 / 1300. Bids (Yuan per TEU per
km) Red/Blue/Green — Chongqing 7.50/9.00/8.60, Beijing 6.40/8.30/8.00, Nanjing
8.80/8.90/9.20, Shijiazhuang 7.80/9.20/8.40.]

Sanity check (ungraded) — Total expected weekly demand: 74 (15+20+26+13).

Part 1 — No capacity limits. Award all TEUs to the cheapest carrier per lane.
Correct answer: Red = 74, Blue = 0, Green = 0 (every lane to Red).
Total expected weekly cost: 610855
Explanation: With no capacity constraint each lane goes to its lowest-cost bid; in
this instance Red is cheapest on all four lanes, so Red carries the full 74 TEUs.
Build the LP with 12 decision variables (4 lanes × 3 carriers), minimize total
cost, satisfy each lane's demand, integer variables.

Part 2 — Carrier weekly capacities: Red 15, Blue 25, Green 90.
Correct answer (TEUs): Red = 15, Blue = 25, Green = 34.
Total expected weekly cost: 662375
Explanation: Add a capacity constraint per carrier (sum of that carrier's TEUs ≤
capacity). Optimal split: Chongqing 15→Green; Beijing 15→Red and 5→Green; Nanjing
25→Blue and 1→Green; Shijiazhuang 13→Green.

Part 3 — Single-source each lane (one carrier per lane, no splits), keep Part 2
capacities.
Correct answer (lanes): Red = 1, Blue = 0, Green = 3.
Total expected weekly cost: 670475
Explanation: Add binary variables (1 if a lane is assigned to a carrier, else 0)
and linking constraints forcing each lane to exactly one carrier. Optimum: Red 1
lane, Blue 0, Green 3.

Part 4 — At least two carriers per lane, at least 5 loads per carrier per lane;
keep Part 2 capacities.
Correct answer (lanes): Red = 2, Blue = 2, Green = 4.
Total expected weekly cost: 669825
Explanation: Use binary assignment variables with linking constraints, plus a
constraint requiring ≥2 carriers per lane and ≥5 loads on any carrier/lane that is
used. Optimum: Red 2 lanes, Blue 2 lanes, Green 4 lanes.

Graded Assignment 3: GetFit (Pilates Power procurement)
(Minimize procurement cost for 5 SKUs — W32A, Y33A, W32B, X33B, Z32A — across
suppliers A–D subject to per-SKU demand and per-supplier total capacity. 20
decision variables = 5 SKUs × 4 suppliers, integer.)

[Captured instance — units needed: W32A 34000, Y33A 5000, W32B 27000, X33B 23000,
Z32A 30000. Supplier capacities: A 51200, B 4500, C 13800, D 52500.]

Part 1 — Minimum total procurement cost
Correct answer: 33781800
Correct answer (units purchased): Supplier A = 51200; Supplier D = 49500.
Explanation: LP with 20 variables, minimize total cost, meet each SKU's demand,
respect each supplier's capacity, integer. Optimal cost 33,781,800.

Part 2 — Level-of-service-adjusted cost (normalize to Supplier A's 92%)
Correct answer: 33781800
Explanation: LOS: A 92%, B/C/D 97%. Cost per % of service depends on SKU (W32A 10,
Y33A 2, W32B 1, X33B 3, Z32A 8). Normalize every supplier's effective unit cost to
Supplier A's 92% baseline, then minimize the LOS-adjusted total cost with the same
demand and capacity constraints. Optimal cost 33,781,800.

Part 3 — Supplier E supplies exactly 20% of all units (use ONLY Part 1 info)
Correct answer (minimum total procurement cost): 33607600
Correct answer (revenue for Supplier E): 5405200
Explanation: Total units needed = 119,000, so Supplier E must supply 20% =
23,800 parts (any mix). E's prices: W32A 555, Y33A 120, W32B 35, X33B 255, Z32A
359. Add E as a 5th supplier (25 variables) with the equality constraint that E's
total = 23,800, minimize total cost. Optimal cost 33,607,600; Supplier E's revenue
(its units × its prices) = 5,405,200.
