Course description

15.C57 Optimization (also listed as 6.C57, 1.C57, and IDS.C57) is a graduate-level introduction to optimization methods, offered through MIT's Common Ground for Computing Education — a set of computing subjects taught jointly by several departments (here departments 1, 6, 15, and IDSS) to educate students who are "bilingual" in both computing and their own discipline. Optimization sits in the prescriptive-analytics layer of data science: where descriptive analytics asks "what happened?" and predictive analytics asks "what will happen?", optimization asks "what should I do?", turning data into decisions across complex problems with many interdependent decisions and interrelated constraints.

The course covers the methods at the core of four areas: linear optimization (geometry, the simplex algorithm, duality, and network flows), integer optimization (modeling techniques and branch-and-cut), optimization under uncertainty (robust, adaptive, and dynamic-programming approaches), and non-linear optimization (gradient descent, Newton's method, stochastic gradient descent, duality, and algorithms for constrained problems). It is organized around four objectives: modeling (translating practical decision problems into well-structured mathematical models), theory (the mathematical and geometric structure of a formulation and what it implies for scalability), algorithms (the technology inside modern solvers, plus designing algorithms when off-the-shelf solvers fall short), and practice (building decision-support tools and turning results into recommendations). Lectures are complemented by computational implementation in Julia using the JuMP modeling language. The two reference texts are Bertsimas & Tsitsiklis, Introduction to Linear Optimization (Athena Scientific, 1997) — cited as "BT" — and Boyd & Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization (Cambridge University Press, 2004) — cited as "BV"; all required content is self-contained in the lecture and recitation notes. The course is taught by Swati Gupta and Saurabh Amin. Assessment is five homework assignments (20%), two midterms (20% each), a term project (30%), and engagement (10%).
